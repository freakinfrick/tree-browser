# The Nine: origin of the Ringwraiths

Filed by: the Keeper of the Roster, Barad-dûr, TA 1600 (re-issued TA 3019).
Ticket: NINE-9. Status: CLOSED WITH THE TOWER (3019-03-25).

The rhyme says "Nine for Mortal Men doomed to die." The Nine were kings and
great sorcerers who took Sauron's rings and, one by one, fell. See
`the-ring/rings-of-power.txt` for the full verse and the other ring counts
(Three for the Elves, Seven for the Dwarves, One for the Dark Lord).

## The deal

- a ring of power: great wealth, great power, great fear.
- terms: the rings bind their bearers to Sauron. The bearers do not read the
  terms.
- addendum (later): "they did not read the terms" — because the terms were
  written in the Black Speech, and the rings do not ship with a gloss.

## The origin (who the Nine were)

- Three of the Nine were lords of the fallen island of Númenor — great
  warriors and sorcerers who had already bent the knee to the shadow.
- Khamûl was of the East: a king among the Easterlings, second to the
  Witch-king, and the one most awake in daylight.
- The Witch-king himself was once a Númenórean lord; he founded the realm of
  Angmar in the North to wage war on Arnor. That is where he earned the name
  *Witch-king of Angmar*. See `15_mordor/minas-morgul/`.
- The other six left no name to any record. The rings took their names first.

## The fall

- They did not die — they *faded*, stretched into the shadow world, until
  they became wraiths: the Nazgûl, neither living nor dead, slaves to the
  One.
- They see the shadow world, not this one; in daylight they are blind; they
  fear fire and running water and the name of Elbereth.
- Their chief is the Witch-king of Angmar (`15_mordor/minas-morgul/`,
  `14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md`).
- "no living man may hinder me" — the Witch-king, Pelennor Fields, 3019-03-15.
  "I am no man." — Éowyn of Rohan, same field, same hour. The roster was
  updated to 8 shortly after; to 0 when the Ring went into the fire.
