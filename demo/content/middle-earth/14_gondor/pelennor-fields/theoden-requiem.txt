THÉODEN, KING OF ROHAN
fell upon the Pelennor, 3019-03-15
not in a hall, but in the field, at the head of his riders
(record of the House of Eorl, filed by the Marshal's clerk;
see `the-charge.md` for the charge he led, and `battle-report.md`
for the field as a whole.)

    Out of doubt, out of dark, to the day's rising
    he rode singing in the sun, sword unsheathing.
    Hope he rekindled, and in hope ended;
    over death, over dread, over doom lifted
    out of loss, out of life, unto long glory.

he is laid in Rath Dínen, in the Hallows of Gondor,
with the kings he would not see fall alone.
his horse Snowmane lies where he fell, under a mound
that reads: FAITHFUL SERVANT YET MASTER'S BANE.

Rohan does not bury its kings; it rides them home.

---

## The lament, as it is recorded

> "Where is the horse and the rider? Where is the horn that was blowing?
> Where is the helm and the hauberk, and the bright hair flowing?"
> — the lament of the Rohirrim, recited by Aragorn as he walked the field
> at night, Pelennor, 3019-03-15

The clerk notes the lament is older than Rohan's walls, and older than its
horses, and that it had never before been said over a king who still had
riders left to say it.

## His last words (attributed)

> "Mourn not overmuch. Mighty was the fallen, meet was his ending."
> — Théoden, to Éowyn, as he lay under Snowmane, Pelennor, 3019-03-15

He spoke them to his sister-daughter and then named Éomer his heir, and
died before the field was won.

## Éomer's grief (recorded later)

> "Mourn not overmuch" — but the Marshal did, and led the host into the
> field's own cry of "Death!" before the day was out, grief and war-cry
> being the same word in Rohan on that morning.
> — the Marshal's clerk, from Éomer's conduct after the King fell

When Éomer found Éowyn lying as if dead beside the King, the clerk records
that he cast his sword from him and the host thought him slain of grief.
The cry that followed was taken up by the whole field.
