# The last letter

**To:** Fimbrethil (Wandlimb the lightfooted), wherever the gardens are now
**From:** Fangorn, at the edge of the forest
**Written:** every Age, the same letter, a little longer each time
**Delivered:** not yet · **Cross-refs:** `missing-persons.csv` (MP-0001),
`sightings.log`, `search-index.json`

---

Fimbrethil,

I am writing the letter again. The last one was not sent, because there
was nowhere to send it, and I have kept it, and this one is the same
letter with an Age added to the end.

The forest is well. It was not well for a while; a wizard to the south was
cutting it, and we went and asked him to stop, and he has stopped. I will
not tell you more than that. It was the hastiest thing I have done since
the day you went east, and I would do it again.

I have walked east, and south, and into the Brown Lands where your gardens
were. There is nothing there now that you would call a garden. I have
asked the Men who live near it, and some of them remember that the corn
was taught to them by tall women who walked like trees, and that is all.

Two small people came into the forest not long ago. They were in a hurry,
as their kind are, but they listened. They have promised to look for you
in their own country, which is full of gardens. One of their cousins
thinks he may have seen someone tall walking in the north. I do not
think it was you. But I have written it down.

The young ones ask whether the Entwives will come back. I tell them the
truth: I do not know. I tell them also the other truth, which is that the
old songs say we will meet again, when we have both lost everything, and
that it may be in a land further west than any I have walked.

I would rather it were sooner. But I am an Ent, and I can wait.

Your beechen leaf is still in my keeping. The apples you planted by the
eaves are still coming up, one or two a year, far from where you left them.

— Fangorn

*Filed, not sent. The next copy is to be written at the turn of the Age.*
