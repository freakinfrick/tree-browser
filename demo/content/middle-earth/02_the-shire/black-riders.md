# The Black Riders in the Shire

**Filed by:** Shirriff Logging Office, Hobbiton ·
**Date:** Third Age 3018-09-26 (compiled after the fact) ·
**Ticket:** SHIRE-RIDER-001 · **Severity:** high, unresolved for the
duration of the search ·
**Cross-refs:** `04_bree/weathertop/incident-report.md`,
`05_rivendell/ford-of-bruinen/flood.log`,
`15_mordor/barad-dur/nazgul-roster.csv`.

**Date:** Third Age 3018, early autumn · **Subject:** nine cloaked riders,
asking for a name · **Name sought:** "Baggins".

## Timeline

- 3018-09-22 — the Riders came over the Brandywine and into the Shire at
  dusk, asking the way to Hobbiton. They entered through Buckland, where
  the gate-watchers were asleep or bought ("we saw nothing, and were paid
  to see less" — the gate-warden of Bucklebury, 3018-09-23, statement later
  retracted).
- 3018-09-23 — the Gaffer (Sam's father), questioned at Bag End, said Frodo
  had gone — and then regretted saying anything at all. "I told him Mr.
  Baggins had gone to Buckland, and then I wished I hadn't spoken, for the
  cold came off him like a river." — the Gaffer, Bag End doorstep,
  3018-09-23 (disputed by the Gaffer the next morning; he now maintains he
  told the Rider nothing whatever).
- 3018-09-25 — Farmer Maggot, questioned at his own gate, told the Rider to
  be off, and threatened to set the dogs on him. The Rider, to everyone's
  surprise, went. "He offered me gold for 'Baggins', and I offered him
  Grip, Fang, and Wolf." — Farmer Maggot, the Marish, 3018-09-25.
- 3018-09-26 — the Riders reached the Shire on the very night Frodo and the
  others were leaving it. The whole escape was run on a margin of hours.

## What the Riders were

Not men on horses. The Nine — kings of Men given rings, now wraiths, and
their horses were bred in Mordor (`../15_mordor/barad-dur/nazgul-roster.csv`).
They carried the blade that later found Frodo on Weathertop
(`../04_bree/weathertop/incident-report.md`).

## What the Shire did

Nothing heroic. The Shire does not do heroic. It did what it always does:
closed its doors, minded its business, and gossiped about the strange folk
in black — and by doing so, slowed the Riders just enough.

## Note

The Shire was never safe from the Riders. It was only *unreadable* to them:
a land of small people in small holes, none of whom were where the Riders
expected. The Ring moved through the Shire like a needle through hay, and
the hay was the only defence anyone had.

## Addendum (filed 3018-10-22)

The search moved east. A rider was later driven from the Ford of Bruinen
and destroyed in the flood (`05_rivendell/ford-of-bruinen/flood.log`, entry
"3018-10-20 — all Nine committed to the crossing"). The Shire's own log was
closed the same week: no Baggins found, no doors forced, no record of what
the Riders actually wanted. The ranger on watch at Sarn Ford filed the
final line: "they were not looking for a house. they were looking for a
ring, and they nearly caught it in the Marish."
