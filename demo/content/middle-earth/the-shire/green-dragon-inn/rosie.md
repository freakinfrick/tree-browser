# Rosie Cotton: bar staff, and the reason for everything

**Filed by:** the taproom ledger, the Green Dragon, Bywater (kept, in part,
by the subject, which is why the handwriting on the staff row is the
prettiest in `tab.csv`)
**Reference:** STAFF-ROSIE-COTTON
**Cross-refs:** `tab.csv`, `songs.txt`, `the-shire/red-book-of-westmarch.md`

**Post:** taproom, the Green Dragon (`tab.csv`, the "staff" row)
**Later:** Mrs. Samwise Gamgee; mother of thirteen; keeper of the Shire.

## Record

- Rosie pulled pints at the Dragon while Sam pulled weeds at Bag End. He
  rehearsed what to say to her for a decade and said none of it until the
  very end.
- She was not in the quest. This is the point. The quest was to get the
  world to a place where people like Rosie could just *be*.
- She sang the Man in the Moon song in the common room on the nights the
  landlord allowed it, and on the nights he didn't she hummed the chorus
  behind the bar (`songs.txt`, "the Man in the Moon one").

## The payoff

- Sam returns from Mordor, walks into the Dragon, and this time he says it.
- They marry; the last chapter of the Red Book (`the-shire/red-book-of-westmarch.md`)
  is the part where the hero comes home and there is a girl at the bar who
  has been waiting.

## Note

Rosie never once calls Sam "Mr. Frodo's gardener." She calls him "Sam."
That, more than any crown or any battle, is the ending the whole thing was
for.
