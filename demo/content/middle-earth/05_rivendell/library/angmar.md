# Angmar and the fall of the North-kingdom

**Subject:** the Witch-king's long war against Arnor
**Shelf:** history, Third Age (early) · **Note:** the archives are thin; the
kingdom is gone.
**Filed by:** the Rivendell library, from Gondorian campaign records and the
lament of the Rangers · **Date of the fall:** TA 1975 (Battle of Fornost)

## What happened

- While Gondor watched the south, the Witch-king — then merely the chief of
  the Nazgûl, not yet openly Sauron's general — built the realm of Angmar in
  the far north and made war on the three kingdoms the North-kingdom had
  split into.
- One by one, Rhudaur, then Cardolan, then Arthedain, fell. The last king of
  Arnor, Arvedui, fled to the ice and drowned; two of the palantíri sank
  with him (`palantiri/registry.md`).

## The counterstroke

- In TA 1975 the Men of Gondor arrived at last — Eärnur at their head, with
  Círdan's elves from Lindon and Glorfindel — and destroyed Angmar at the
  Battle of Fornost. But it was too late for Arnor.
- The Witch-king fled south, mocked by Glorfindel, who spoke the prophecy
  that saved everyone: *"Far off yet is his doom, and not by the hand of man
  will he fall."* (see `14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md` for the
  collection of that debt.)

## The debt matures (addendum, filed 3019-03-15)

Glorfindel's words were a receipt, not a threat. They were redeemed at the
Pelennor, by one who was no man, and by a hobbit's Westernesse blade. See
`14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md`. The Witch-king spent the
intervening fourteen hundred years as the Warden of Minas Morgul, where the
green light burns (`15_mordor/minas-morgul/green-light.md`); it went dark the
day he fell, and has not been reported lit since.

## A footnote on the man who chased him

Eärnur, last King of Gondor, rode to Minas Morgul in TA 2050 to answer the
Witch-king's taunt and was never seen again. The prophecy did not cover
kings. It did not cover Eärnur. It should have.

## Why it matters

The Witch-king practised on the North-kingdom for a thousand years. The
Rangers of the North — Aragorn's people — are what is left of it, and the
Shire was founded in its ruins, under its protection. The Ring left the
Shire, but the Shire never forgot whose land it was.
