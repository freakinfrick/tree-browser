# The Two Towers: a bibliographic note

The second volume of the Red Book is titled "The Two Towers". The record
does not say which two, and this has been argued ever since.

**Filed by:** the Rivendell library's cataloguer, long after the fact ·
**Date of filing:** Fourth Age · **Status:** catalogued under all pairs, and
under none.

## Candidates

1. Orthanc and Barad-dûr (the tower of Saruman, the tower of Sauron).
2. Minas Tirith and Minas Morgul (the tower of the guard, the tower of
   sorcery).
3. Cirith Ungol and Orthanc (the two towers of the third book's action).

## The cataloguer's ruling

The library files the title under **Orthanc and Minas Morgul** — the two
towers the second volume actually stands between: the tower Saruman raised
in the west (`isengard/orthanc/`), and the tower the Enemy had taken in the
east (`mordor/minas-morgul/`). This ruling is marked *(disputed)* in the
margin, because a later hand has written "Barad-dûr" next to it, and a still
later hand has written "Minbar", which is not a tower in any of the three
ages and has been left in place as a warning about trusting the margins.

## The author's own view (from a later letter)

Tolkien, asked which two, pointed at Orthanc and Barad-dûr, then said the
title could stand for whichever pair the reader liked — which is the correct
answer to most questions about the book.

## The towers themselves

- **Orthanc** — the black spire of Isengard, built of stone that would not
  break. Flooded, not demolished (`isengard/orthanc/`).
- **Minas Morgul** — once Minas Ithil, the Tower of the Moon; taken, renamed,
  and let go dark (`mordor/minas-morgul/`).

## Record note

The machine does not resolve ambiguity. The machine files it under both, and
under none. The towers themselves are in `isengard/` and `mordor/`; the
contents of the One are in `the-ring/`.
