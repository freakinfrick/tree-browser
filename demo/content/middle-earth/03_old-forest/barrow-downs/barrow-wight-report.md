# Barrow-wights: incident report

**Filed by:** Frodo Baggins · **Date filed:** 3018-09-29, at the Prancing Pony,
Bree, the evening after · **Incident ID:** BARROW-3018-09-28
**Site:** the Barrow-downs, east of the Old Forest
**Date:** 3018-09-28, fog, then dark (the day they left Tom's house)
**Severity:** SEV-1 (the Ring-bearer nearly sacrificed)
**Refs:** `03_old-forest/tom-bombadil.md`, `14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md`

## What happened

- A fog came down on the Downs. The hobbits were separated.
- Frodo woke in a barrow: cold, dark, with a long arm reaching for a sword.
- The wight had laid the hobbits out in treasure, a blade at each throat,
  and was singing the end of them.

## Resolution

- Frodo did not run. He cut the wight's hand, and called for Tom.
- Tom came; the wight was banished with a song; the barrow was emptied and
  the treasure given to the hobbits.

> "Cold be hand and heart and bone, and cold be sleep under stone: never
> more to wake on stony bed, never, till the Sun fails and the Moon is dead."
> — the Barrow-wight, in the barrow, 3018-09-28 (as remembered by Frodo;
> the cadence, not the words, is filed here)

> "Get out, you old Wight! Vanish in the sunlight! ... out into the barren
> lands far beyond the mountains!"
> — Tom Bombadil, banishing the wight, 3018-09-28

## Loot recovered

- four long daggers of Westernesse — blades wound about with spells against
  the Witch-king. One of them (Merry's) later undid the Witch-king's
  invulnerability at the Pelennor
  (`14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md`).

## Follow-up: the provenance that mattered

The blades were not random treasure. They were forged by the Men of
Westernesse, ages ago, expressly against the chief of the Nazgûl — the one
the hobbits had not yet met, and would meet at the Pelennor. Merry's blade
found the back of the Witch-king's knee at the exact moment the prophecy
"no living man may hinder me" met its test case, and the blow Éowyn landed
would not have landed otherwise. Filed here so the provenance of that blow
is never lost (`14_gondor/pelennor-fields/eowyn-vs-witchking.md`).

## Lesson

The wights guard the treasure of a dead kingdom. The treasure was the only
part worth keeping — and it took the whole story to prove it.
